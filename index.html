DRG
  • Creation (claim/part): Assigned by grouper to institutional inpatient (UB-04/837I) claims — Type of Bill 11x, paid under Medicare Part A (IPPS). Medicaid IP claims in TAF also use UB-04 (state policies vary). Inputs: principal DX, procedures, CC/MCC, age/sex, discharge status.
  • Input to bundling/VBC: Payment unit for IPPS (Part A); anchors episodes in DRG-based cost measures and readmissions programs (HRRP); state Medicaid programs may use DRG-based IP payment.
  • Used with other data: Wage index, case-mix, outlier thresholds, discharge/transfer status, transfer rules.
HCPCS (CPT Level I / Level II)
  • Creation (claim/part): Reported on professional (CMS-1500/837P) and hospital outpatient (UB-04/837I, TOB 13x) claims in Medicare Part B; also used in MA (C) encounters and Medicaid (TAF). Level I maintained by AMA (CPT); Level II by CMS.
  • Input to bundling/VBC: Drives OPPS/APC grouping (Part B), MPFS pricing (B), and episode/cost measures in value-based programs; similar logic in MA/Medicaid payment edits.
  • Used with other data: Status indicators (OPPS B), units, modifiers (25/59/JW/JZ), revenue codes, NDC for drugs (especially Part D contexts).
HCC — CMS Risk Adjustment
  • Creation (claim/part): Derived annually from ICD-10-CM diagnoses on institutional (UB-04/837I: A/B) and professional (1500/837P: B) claims across settings; computes RAF for Medicare Advantage (Part C); some Medicaid programs adopt HCC-like risk models.
  • Input to bundling/VBC: Adjusts MA (C) capitation and ACO benchmarks; used for risk stratification and performance attribution.
  • Used with other data: Demographics (age/sex/dual/disability), model interactions, encounter completeness rules to produce final RAF.
APC
  • Creation (claim/part): Assigned to hospital outpatient (UB-04/837I) claims — TOB 13x, paid under Medicare Part B (OPPS). State Medicaid programs may use OPPS-like rules. Built from CPT/HCPCS + status indicators (includes packaging and C-APC logic).
  • Input to bundling/VBC: Payment unit for OPPS (B); packaging creates de facto ambulatory bundles; used in outpatient episode/cost measures in VBP contexts.
  • Used with other data: OPPS conversion factor, wage index, multiple-procedure discounting, device/drug pass-through status, required modifiers.
Claim Forms & Type of Bill Codes
UB-04 / Form CMS-1450 → 837I Institutional claim form/transaction. Used for Medicare A/B facility billing to MACs and by many Medicaid State Agencies for institutional claims. Setting indicator: Type of Bill (TOB).
CMS-1500 → 837P Professional (non-institutional) claim form/transaction. Used for Medicare Part B physician/supplier claims and Medicaid professional billing. Stewarded by NUCC. Setting indicator: Place of Service (POS).
TOB 11x Hospital Inpatient (e.g., 111 = admit-through-discharge). Claims paid under Medicare Part A — IPPS, DRG-based payment. Medicaid institutional IP claims in TAF also carry TOB.
TOB 13x Hospital Outpatient (e.g., 131 = outpatient claim). Claims paid under Medicare Part B — OPPS; APC-based payment applies.
Place of Service (POS) 2-digit code on professional claims (CMS-1500/837P) identifying the care setting. Required for Medicare Part B; widely used by Medicaid and other payers.

Mini-Glossary
BENE_ID Encrypted CCW Beneficiary ID. Stable, linkable key across CCW files for Medicare FFS (A/B), MA encounters (C), Part D (D), and CCW-linked Medicaid (TAF). Use: link claims/encounters/PDEs to enrollment and each other; build denominators.
CLM_ID Claim header identifier. Revenue-center/line segments appear beneath the header. Exists in Medicare FFS (A/B), MA encounters (C), and analogous constructs in Medicaid TAF (institutional/professional).
CLM_FROM_DT / CLM_THRU_DT "Statement covers from/thru" dates. Present in Medicare FFS (A/B) and MA encounters (C). Part D uses fill date variables; TAF carries service dates for Medicaid.
CLM_PMT_AMT Claim-level Medicare payment amount on institutional (UB-04/837I) claims. Paired with revenue-center line amounts for granular cost analysis.
MedPAR Medicare Part A stay-level roll-up from inpatient hospital and SNF claims. Convenient for admissions/LOS analytics; not line-level.
RAF (Risk Adjustment Factor) The numeric risk score produced by the HCC model. Combines HCC scores and demographic factors (age/sex/dual/disability). Applied to MA capitation benchmarks and ACO financial benchmarks.
NDC (National Drug Code) 11-digit drug identifier on Part D Prescription Drug Events (PDEs) and Medicaid pharmacy claims. Identifies the specific drug, strength, and package size.
TAF (T-MSIS Analytic Files) Research-optimized Medicaid claims/encounters (institutional and non-institutional), eligibility, and provider files derived from T-MSIS. Access via ResDAC/CMS.
NPI (National Provider Identifier) 10-digit identifier for individual providers and organizations. Present in Medicare A/B/C/D contexts; Medicaid TAF has parallel provider keys. Use to link claims to POS file, HCRIS, and MIPS performance data.

National Context: OBBBA's Core Provisions

P.L. 119-21 (H.R. 1) is a budget reconciliation law, not healthcare-specific legislation — its healthcare provisions are four of many titles. Two of the biggest compounding factors in 2026 (the expired enhanced ACA premium tax credits and the broader MA risk-adjustment/RADV litigation) are not OBBBA provisions; they are separate, coincidentally-timed policy events. This section covers OBBBA's actual text first, then flags what compounds it.

Medicaid Work Requirements
  • What it requires: 80 hrs/month of work, work/education program, or community service (or a $580+/month income equivalent in some state implementations) as a condition of Medicaid eligibility for the ACA expansion population, ages 19-64.
  • Effective: By January 1, 2027 nationally, across the 40 expansion states + DC. CMS published the implementing interim final rule (CMS-2454-IFC, "Community Engagement Requirement for Certain Individuals") June 1, 2026. Self-declaration of exemptions is permitted for 2027; supporting documentation is required starting 2028. Applicable enrollees move to 6-month (from annual) redeterminations.
  • Early movers: Nebraska, Montana, Iowa, and Arkansas are enforcing ahead of the federal deadline on their own state timelines — CMS's own interim final rule names all four (detail in Archetypes, below).
  • CBO estimate: ~10M combined coverage loss by 2034 across OBBBA's Medicaid and marketplace provisions — see Affected Populations for sourcing caveats.
Immigrant Eligibility Changes
  • §71109 — narrows Medicaid eligibility for lawfully present immigrants: The Medicaid-side companion to §71301/§71302 below — moves an estimated ~200,000 Californians from full-scope to partial-scope (emergency-only) Medi-Cal, per UC Berkeley Labor Center. This is the provision that drives FQHC/safety-net revenue exposure for immigrant-heavy caseloads, distinct from the marketplace-subsidy provisions below.
  • §71302 — five-year rule (marketplace, not Medicaid): Eliminates ACA premium tax credit (PTC) eligibility for lawfully present immigrants below 100% FPL who are ineligible for Medicaid because of immigration status (including those in their first 5 years in the U.S.). Effective for tax years after December 31, 2025 — already in effect. Affects Covered California marketplace subsidies, not Medi-Cal.
  • §71301 — narrowed "eligible alien" definition (marketplace, not Medicaid): Restricts PTC-eligible immigration categories to lawful permanent residents, Cuban/Haitian entrants, and COFA migrants. Refugees, asylees, and Temporary Protected Status holders lose PTC eligibility entirely. Effective January 1, 2027.
Premium Tax Credit Repayment Caps Eliminated
  • §71305: Repeals the repayment caps in §36B(f)(2)(B) that, through 2025, limited how much excess advance PTC a household under 400% FPL had to repay if their actual income came in higher than estimated (caps varied by income tier and filing status, from $375 up to $1,625 single / $3,250 other filers at the highest tier below 400% FPL).
  • Effective: Tax year 2026 onward. Any marketplace enrollee whose actual income exceeds their estimate — for any reason, at any income level — now owes back the full excess credit, dollar for dollar. This is the "uncapped clawback" risk referenced throughout this tab.
Provider Tax / SDP Caps & Rural Health Transformation Fund
  • Provider tax freeze, then phase-down: A nationwide freeze effective October 1, 2026 locks all states' provider-tax rates at their July 4, 2025 levels; from FFY2028 (Oct 2027) the allowable ceiling then phases down from 6% to 3.5% in expansion states specifically, through 2032.
  • State-directed payment (SDP) caps: Limits SDPs to 100% of Medicare rates in expansion states, 110% in non-expansion states, phasing down starting January 1, 2028 — reducing a major supplemental-financing lever hospitals and states have used since 2021. Note the revenue-impact window for SDPs runs mainly 2028-2032, not 2026-2028 — later than the provider-tax freeze/phase-down above. Nursing facilities and ICF/IDD provider taxes are exempt from the 6%-to-3.5% phase-down and keep the 6% safe harbor.
  • Rural Health Transformation Fund: $50B, distributed FY2026-2030 via CMS-approved state transformation blueprints. Widely expected by rural-health analysts (National Rural Health Association, hospital associations) not to fully offset the combined revenue loss from coverage attrition and the provider-tax/SDP caps above.

What compounds OBBBA (not part of the law itself): The IRA-era enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired December 31, 2025 without congressional renewal — subsidized marketplace enrollees' average annual premium roughly doubled, from $888 to $1,904, and the "subsidy cliff" at 400% FPL returned for everyone. Separately, MA risk-adjustment (HCC V28) and RADV litigation is compressing plan margins for reasons unrelated to OBBBA (see Top Shifts, Ontology tab). Both land in the same 2026-2027 window as OBBBA's provisions and are treated as compounding catalysts throughout this tab, not attributed to OBBBA itself.

Individual Mandate Variation: State Penalty Rules for 2026

The federal individual mandate penalty was zeroed out by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act; five jurisdictions have since enacted their own. As the federal enhanced-subsidy expiration raises marketplace premiums nationally, these state penalties interact directly with affordability in the states that have them — going uninsured now carries both a higher unsubsidized premium risk and, in five places, a direct tax penalty.

Disproportionately Affected Populations

Medicaid / Marketplace Coverage Loss
  • CBO's cumulative scoring across OBBBA's Medicaid and marketplace provisions estimates roughly 10 million more people uninsured by 2034. Component estimates vary by provision and by which CBO score vintage is cited — treat 10M as an order-of-magnitude anchor, not a precise figure, and check for updated CBO scoring each refresh cycle.
Subsidy Clawback Exposure
  • OBBBA §71305 eliminated repayment caps starting tax year 2026 (see National Context). Anyone whose actual income exceeds their marketplace estimate — a raise, a spouse's new job, a good freelance year — now owes back the full advance premium tax credit with no cap, at any income level. This is a new, uncapped downside risk distinct from the separate 400% FPL subsidy-cliff issue.
ESRD & Disability Dual-Eligibles
  • CMS's dual-enrollment data brief shows disability-based Medicare eligibility present in roughly 38-43% of dual-eligible (Medicare-Medicaid) beneficiaries (2006-2015 series). A second independent verification pass could not locate a directly comparable, CMS-sourced Medicare-only-beneficiary figure to compute a disparity ratio — treat any specific multiplier (this project has used both "2x" and "4.7x" in different drafts) as unverified until a single CMS source with both figures is located. What is confirmed: dual-eligibles carry a materially higher disability-based eligibility share and are simultaneously exposed to Medicaid work-requirement and eligibility churn on the Medicaid side of their coverage.
Immigrant Eligibility Narrowing
  • Lawfully present immigrants under 100% FPL in their first 5 years in the U.S. lose PTC eligibility for tax years after 2025 (already in effect). Refugees, asylees, and TPS holders lose PTC-eligible "alien" status entirely starting Jan 1, 2027 — a population previously treated as marketplace-eligible under ACA rules since 2014.

The 2026–2030 Landscape: Coverage Loss Amid Accelerating Cost Growth

OBBBA is not an isolated event — it compounds an existing cost trend. PwC's 2027 medical cost trend survey (published June 11, 2026) puts Group at 9% and Individual at 8.5% — the highest commercial trend in 17 years, independent of anything in OBBBA. Aon's "9.5%" figure needs a year caveat: this project has two differently-dated Aon sources — one from Sept 2025 headlined "9.5% in 2026" and one from Aug 2026 headlined "9.5% in 2027" — and could not resolve in this pass whether that's a genuine coincidence across two separate annual reports or a citation mix-up; verify against Aon's original report before citing a specific year. Mercer's 8.2% and WTW's 11.1% figures circulate widely for this period but were not independently re-verified against each firm's own published report in this pass; cite with that caveat. Three-State Drill-Down: California, Montana, Georgia

Three distinct archetypes, chosen for contrast: CA (Expansion + Litigant, largest Medicaid program in the U.S.), MT (Early Adopter, rapid state-initiated rollout), GA (Waiver-Only/Non-Expansion, the only state with a multi-year work-requirement track record). Each card covers exposure, action plan, strengths/weaknesses, tipping point, cost-shift cascade, and incentive structure.

California — Expansion + Litigant
  • Exposure: Largest Medicaid program in the U.S. (Medi-Cal). Three distinct, non-conflicting DHCS/LAO estimates, not competing numbers: DHCS puts 4.8M enrollees subject to the work requirement (2.2M expected exempt), with 1.1M as its own central estimate of coverage loss and up to 3.4M as a higher-end/worst-case scenario (modeled on other states' early work-requirement experience); the LAO separately projects ~2M more Californians uninsured by end of 2030 from OBBBA plus state policy changes combined (a broader net figure spanning Medi-Cal, marketplace, and immigrant-eligibility effects, netting out those who find other coverage — LAO attributes roughly 90% of it to OBBBA). Effective Jan 1, 2027; 6-month redeterminations from March 2027; $580/month income accepted as an alternative to 80 hrs/month.
  • Action plan: DHCS "Tracking Federal Impact" public hub; CA is a co-plaintiff (not lead) in Commonwealth of Massachusetts et al. v. Oz (MA is the named lead plaintiff; 25 states + DC total, filed June 2026) over the medically-frail exemption rule — a federal judge denied the coalition's preliminary injunction on July 29, 2026, so the rule remains in effect while litigation continues — and co-leads a separate 21-AG + 1-governor suit (filed N.D. Cal.) against the 2027 Notice of Benefit and Payment Parameters. CHCF publishes provider/plan implementation guidance.
  • Strengths vs. weaknesses: Mature statewide MCO infrastructure and existing CalAIM 1115-waiver social-needs screening could double as an exemption-documentation rail (strength) — but CA faces its own concurrent state budget shortfalls even as DHCS's own reporting flags implementation capacity risk (weakness), and CA's sheer population size means even a small procedural-failure rate produces large absolute coverage-loss numbers.
  • Tipping point: If CA's Jan 2027 rollout mirrors early-adopter states' experience — where paperwork/documentation failure, not actual non-work, drives most disenrollment — the realized number could exceed DHCS's 3.4M at-risk estimate.
  • Cost-shift cascade: Federal Medicaid match reductions shift onto the state general fund, county eligibility offices, and public/safety-net hospitals (e.g. Zuckerberg SF General, LAC+USC, Highland Hospital) as the ultimate backstop.
  • Incentive structure: Counties and MCOs are financially incentivized to minimize procedural disenrollment — counties to avoid downstream uncompensated-care cost, MCOs because capitation revenue depends on retained enrollment.
Montana — Early Adopter
  • Exposure: Enforcement begins July 1, 2026 — six months ahead of the federal deadline. A 3-month non-enforcement grace period (Jul-Oct 2026) precedes actual disenrollment risk.
  • Action plan: DPHHS launched a public online resource hub (March 2026) and is "moving forward... despite questions" (per its own framing in state press coverage) about system readiness.
  • Strengths vs. weaknesses: Early move gives MT (and CMS) real operational data before most states reach their 2027 deadline (strength) — but the Montana Budget & Policy Center and Montana Hospital Association have both publicly flagged eligibility-system readiness gaps (weakness).
  • Tipping point: First disenrollment wave, December 31, 2026. DPHHS's own estimate is ~13,000 adults (~18% of the expansion population) losing coverage by the 2027 enforcement completion — if GA's experience (procedural loss dominating over genuine non-compliance) repeats, this is a floor, not a ceiling.
  • Cost-shift cascade: Rural Montana hospitals, already thin-margin, absorb uncompensated care first; the state general fund backstops eligibility-system costs.
  • Incentive structure: Moving early lets Montana shape its own implementation terms ahead of a one-size-fits-all federal framework, per its budget-policy-center reporting.
Georgia — Waiver-Only / Non-Expansion
  • Exposure: Pathways to Coverage has run since July 2023 — two-plus years before OBBBA's national requirement — and was extended through Dec 31, 2026 despite its own cost findings.
  • Action plan: State and federal government (the Trump administration extended the program in Sept 2025) have doubled down rather than restructured, despite the GAO's admin-cost findings.
  • Strengths vs. weaknesses: GA is the only state with real multi-year operational data on a Medicaid work-requirement program — genuinely useful evidence as CMS designs the national rollout (strength) — but GAO documented a 2:1 admin-to-care spending ratio ($54.2M vs. $26.1M, 2021-June 2025, ~88% federally funded) and enrollment has run far below target (7,400-9,175 vs. ~246,365 eligible, missing even its own ~25,000 first-year goal per GAO) (weakness). Deloitte's $27M Pathways marketing contract (grown from an initial $10.7M award) produced enrollment still under 3% of the ~246,365 eligible even after the campaign ran.
  • Tipping point: The Dec 31, 2026 extension deadline — if Pathways is folded into the 2027 national framework rather than renewed standalone, GA's cost structure becomes either the template CMS exports or the cautionary tale other states cite to avoid it.
  • Cost-shift cascade: The federal government has covered ~88% of Pathways' administrative spend to date, so the "vendor spending > care" pattern has so far been a federal cost, not a Georgia one — that changes if OBBBA's national framework shifts more administrative burden onto states. The 2:1 admin-to-care ratio is also front-loaded, not a steady state: the administrative share fell from 96.5% of spend in FY2023 to 58.8% in FY2024 as the initial system buildout cost was absorbed — a caution against extrapolating GA's early ratio flatly onto the national rollout's steady-state cost.
  • Incentive structure: State political incentive to preserve Pathways as proof-of-concept for work requirements nationally, even as its own numbers argue against the model's cost-effectiveness.

Limitations & Caveats

  • State case-study numbers are mostly projections, not realized outcomes — evidence grade varies by row (see Archetypes table): confirmed for NE, MT, GA (GAO), and ND (all independently re-verified against state-agency/GAO/tracker sources across multiple research passes); modeled for IA (state Legislative Services Agency projection); insufficient for the Expansion+Waiver archetype (HI/MA/NY/OR/UT) pending a verified state-specific figure. Texas's confirmed application-backlog figure is real but was removed from the archetype table itself, since Texas is a non-expansion state with no work-requirement population — see the note below the Archetypes table.
  • Threading state individual-mandate penalty interactions with the federal enhanced-subsidy expiration (Individual Mandate Variation section) is original synthesis for this repo, not a claim mirrored from any single cited source — treat it as an analytical framing, not an established consensus finding.
  • The Georgia Pathways-to-national-rollout comparison (Case Studies) is illustrative of work-requirement administrative-cost mechanics, but GA's waiver program and OBBBA's national requirement are legally distinct instruments — GA's numbers are a preview, not a direct prediction, of national-rollout costs.
  • This research pass could not independently verify: the spec's original Hawaii 193-minute call-center wait-time figure, or Bay Area advocacy organizations' specific published positions on OBBBA. Both are flagged inline rather than asserted as fact. (Iowa's and North Dakota's figures, previously flagged as unverified here, were confirmed in a later independent verification pass — see Archetypes table. North Dakota was reinstated to that table after initially being dropped in error.)
  • The specific ESRD/disability dual-eligible disparity ratio (this project has at different points used both "2x" and "4.7x") could not be confirmed against a single CMS source pairing both a dual-eligible and a Medicare-only figure — treat any such multiplier as unverified until re-sourced.
  • The CBO ~10M coverage-loss figure is a combined, cross-provision estimate that varies by score vintage and methodology across CBO, KFF, and Georgetown CCF — used here as an order-of-magnitude anchor, not a precise number.
  • Litigation status is a snapshot as of this pass (early September 2026): a preliminary injunction was denied in Massachusetts v.
